## 1. Earthenware/terracotta bodies: composition, grog, firing range, shrinkage (drying vs firing vs total), absorption, temperature sensitivity, shrinkage test and mould scale factor

### Takeaway
Terracotta matures over a narrow, non-linear range, roughly cone 06–04 (≈1000–1060 °C). In that range it is porous (≈10–12 % at cone 04) and has low firing shrinkage, often 3–4 % or less from dry to fired, and some bodies are near 0 or even expand. Most of the total shrinkage is **drying shrinkage**, which can run from about 4.5 % to 12 % depending on the clay.

The only reliable scale factor comes from test bars made from *your* clay, pressed the way you will press it and fired in *the studio's* kiln on *its* programme. Compute the scale factor as **1/(1 − s_total)**, not as 1 + s.

### Cited Findings
**Firing range and non-linear behaviour [T]**
- Terra cotta is "typically fired between cone 06 and 04". Cone 04 is called the sweet spot. "Terra cottas respond in a non-linear fashion to heating beyond cone 04, densifying and melting increasingly rapidly often warping and blistering by cone 01" — [Digitalfire: Terra Cotta](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/terra%20cotta)
- Plainsman BGP terracotta test bars fired at cone 06, 04, 03, 02 and 2 [T] — [Digitalfire picture 1943](https://digitalfire.com/picture/1943):
  - At cone 06 ("1830F/1000C") "it is porous and shrinks very little".
  - Colour deepens around cone 03 ("1950F/1070C").
  - Maximum density is reached at cone 02.
  - Past cone 02 "it becomes unstable, beginning to melt (as indicated by negative shrinkage)".
  - "cone 04, is a good compromise: Adequate strength, good color and low shrinkage."

**Porosity and absorption [T]**
- Porosity is about "10-12%" at cone 04 and about 6 % at cone 02, against "<2%" for stonewares — [Digitalfire: Terra Cotta](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/terra%20cotta)
- Drying shrinkage of terra cottas varies widely: one example had 4.5 % and another 12 % — [Digitalfire: Terra Cotta](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/terra%20cotta)

**Firing shrinkage [T]** — [Digitalfire: Firing Shrinkage](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/firing+shrinkage)
- Dry-to-fired shrinkage is typically "whitewares 7-8%… stonewares about 5-6% and earthenwares 3-4% or less".
- These numbers are dry-to-fired, not wet-to-fired.
- "fired shrinkages are relative within a system, there is no absolute of how much a clay should shrink."

**Drying and fired shrinkage cannot simply be added [T]** — [Digitalfire: Firing Shrinkage](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/firing+shrinkage)
- Example: "6.25 dry shrinkage + 6.66 fired = 12.9 whereas the actual total shrinkage is 12.5%". The true total is "a little less than adding the two together".
- My check: 1 − (1 − 0.0625)(1 − 0.0666) = 12.49 %, which matches.

**Some low-fire bodies grow in firing [T]**
- Plainsman BGP with 30 % dolomite had *negative* fired shrinkage (the bars grew) at cone 05–01 — [Digitalfire: Firing Shrinkage](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/firing+shrinkage)
- The same body still had drying shrinkage above 7 % and 18.7 % absorption.

**Brazilian red-clay data [T]** — Zaccaron et al., *R. Eng. Constr. Civ.* 3(1), 2016 — [UTFPR PDF](https://periodicos.utfpr.edu.br/recc/article/download/6631/4278)
- Two Santa Catarina clays had: SiO₂ 69–71 %, Al₂O₃ 15.6–18.5 %, Fe₂O₃ 3.9–8.9 %, loss on ignition 5.9–6.7 %.
- For red ceramics, citing Dondi (2006):
  - Drying linear shrinkage is considered optimal at 5–8 %, acceptable at 3–10 %.
  - Firing linear shrinkage is optimal below 1.5 %, acceptable at 1.5–3 %.
- Lab firing was 2 °C/min to 900 °C with a 2 h soak.
- Water absorption was about 14 % for the standard mix (derived from "variação de 3,43%… chegando a 17,42%") and 17.42 % with 20 % grog (chamote).
- Grog lowered drying shrinkage.

**Grog (chamote) [T]**
- Grog reduces drying and firing shrinkage. However, "the grog addition can actually weaken the leather hard and dry strength" — [Digitalfire: Grog](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/grog)
- Sculpture bodies typically carry 20–30 % grog. Bodies stay smooth when "the grog is of a narrow range of smaller sizes" and "grog content is below 20%" — [Digitalfire: Grog](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/grog)
- Grog is not a cure for cracking. A body with 10 % added 50–80 mesh grog cracked in a similar pattern to the same body without it, and the grog cut drying shrinkage by only 0.5–0.75 %. "any normal smooth commercial pottery clay will dry without cracking if ware is dried evenly" — [Digitalfire: Drying Ceramics Without Cracks](https://digitalfire.com/article/drying+ceramics+without+cracks)

**Clay choice for vessel flutes [M]**
- Robert Hickman: "You should use a smooth (ungrogged) earthenware clay, although a smooth stoneware clay will work, at the cost of higher shrinkage" — [ocarinamaking.com, How to make a Xun](https://ocarinamaking.com/page/how_to_make_a_xun)
- Pure Ocarinas: most ceramic ocarinas are earthenware, "a type of low-temperature ceramic that remains porous". Vitrified stoneware and porcelain ocarinas are "uncommon, and hard to make due to high shrinkage" — [Pure Ocarinas: materials & finishes](https://pureocarinas.com/ocarina-materials-finish-differences)
- Budrio tradition: a Mignani ocarina is catalogued as made of the reddish terracotta typical of the Bologna area [S] — [Catalogo Patrimonio Emilia-Romagna, scheda Mignani](https://bbcc.regione.emilia-romagna.it/pater/loadcard.do?id_card=71029) (page returned 403; seen only as a search snippet)

**Shrinkage test: ASTM C326 [T]**
- Purpose: to measure drying and firing shrinkage "to enable designers to determine the proper size of mold or die so as to produce a predetermined size of fired ware" — [ASTM C326](https://www.astm.org/Standards/C326.htm)
- A secondary summary says the bars are standard molded bars with reference marks, dried at 110–115 °C to constant mass and then fired — [Infinita Lab C326 summary](https://infinitalab.com/material-testing-service/drying-and-firing-shrinkage-of-ceramic-whiteware-clays-astm-c326/). The full standard is paywalled and I did not verify exact bar dimensions.

**Shrinkage test: Digitalfire SHAB, a practical equivalent [T]** — [Digitalfire SHAB test](https://digitalfire.com/test/shab)
- Bars 4¼" × 1", cut from clay rolled to 3/8" thick.
- Marks impressed exactly **10 cm apart**, measured with calipers to 0.1 mm.
- Bars dried at 80–100 °C, then fired with Orton cones for verification.
- Firing shrinkage = (V1 − V2)/V1 × 100, where V1 is dry length and V2 is fired length.
- Absorption = (V4 − V3)/V3 × 100, from fired weight (V3) and weight after a 5 h boil plus 19 h soak (V4).

**Firing temperatures in Brazilian studio vocabulary [M]**
- Queima de biscoito (bisque firing): "eleva-se a temperatura até 800/900 °C". A careful bisque takes about 8 h, plus about 8 h before the door can be fully opened. The piece stays "porosa e não impermeável" — [Tauá Cerâmica: Os tipos de queima](https://www.tauaceramica.com/post/2016/02/24/os-tipos-de-queima)
- The same page claims about 10 % shrinkage "em seu tamanho e volume". That figure is generic and ambiguous.

**Portuguese terminology**
- "Chamote é argila queimada e moída… para aumentar a resistência e diminuir a retração" — [Ceramista da Casa: dicionário](https://www.ceramistadacasa.com.br/dicionario)
- Engobe: "material composto por argila, fundentes e colorantes… para recobrir a superfície de uma peça em ponto de couro" — [Ceramista da Casa: dicionário](https://www.ceramistadacasa.com.br/dicionario)
- Retração: "Cada massa possui um percentual de retração… é importante realizar testes" — [Ceramista da Casa: dicionário](https://www.ceramistadacasa.com.br/dicionario)
- Brazilian retail example: "argila terracota chamote fino" is sold in 10 kg bags — [Fruto de Arte](https://frutodearte.com.br/products/argila-para-escultura-e-artesanato-terracota-chamote-fino-10-kg) (search listing only)

**Leather-hard (ponto de couro) [T]**
- Leather-hard clay has "approximately 15% moisture content" — [Wikipedia: Leather-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather-hard)

### Inferences
**Mould scale factor**
- Scale factor = **1/(1 − s_total)**, where s_total is linear wet-to-fired shrinkage measured with SHAB/C326-style bars.
- Master dimension = fired target ÷ (1 − s_total). For the 175 mm length:

| s_total | Scale factor | PLA master length | Result if you wrongly use 1 + s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 % | 1.0638 | 186.2 mm | 174.4 mm fired |
| 8 % | 1.0870 | 190.2 mm | 173.9 mm fired |
| 10 % | 1.1111 | 194.4 mm | 173.3 mm fired (≈1 % small, about +17 cents sharp) |
| 12 % | 1.1364 | 198.9 mm | 172.5 mm fired |

- Arithmetic by me. The pitch conversion is in section 7.

**Test protocol adapted to this project** (my own design, based on ASTM C326/SHAB)
- Press 5 or more bars in plaster, from the same clay bag at the same moisture as the ocarina halves.
- Mark 100.0 mm on each bar.
- Measure at four points:
  - (1) wet;
  - (2) **at the moment you would tune** (leather-hard), which is new relative to the standard tests;
  - (3) bone-dry;
  - (4) after firing on the studio's actual programme and cone, ideally in the same load as the ocarinas.
- This gives s_total, used for the master scale, and **r = remaining shrinkage from tuning point to fired**, which predicts the pitch rise.
- Also measure absorption (boil test), so you know how porous the finished instrument will be.

**Why the upper bound still needs a test**
- The Digitalfire and Brazilian data together show firing shrinkage can range from negative to about 3–4 % for terracotta at 900–1060 °C. Drying shrinkage (3–12 %) dominates the total.
- The "upper-bound" rule for the master is sound. But the bound is wide (roughly 5–14 % total), so a single test-bar batch narrows it a lot.

**Choosing a body**
- Hickman's "smooth, ungrogged" advice and Digitalfire's grog findings both point to a **fine terracotta, at most about 10–15 % fine grog (chamote fino)**, for thin (3–5 mm) press-moulded walls with a crisp labium.
- Coarse grog tears labium edges. That last point is my inference and is not directly sourced.

**Keep the peak temperature constant**
- Because terracotta is non-linear above cone 04, a rented kiln that overshoots by 1–2 cones can change shrinkage, and therefore pitch, disproportionately.
- Ask the studio for its exact programme and cone, and use witness cones (cones testemunho) if possible.

### Gaps
- The exact bar dimensions and marking distances in ASTM C326 were not verified; the standard is paywalled.
- I found no published data on how much drying shrinkage remains after leather-hard for any terracotta. It must be measured, as in the protocol above.
- There is no shrinkage data for a specific Goiânia/Goiás red clay. Commercial Brazilian terracotta datasheets were not retrieved.

## 2. Press moulding: clay consistency, slab thickness, wall targets, even pressing, demoulding, warping

### Takeaway
Press a compressed slab of even thickness, rib-compressed and pressed with a soft pad rather than fingertips, into both mould halves.
- Target thickness: about 3–5 mm. Pressed makers' ware uses about 3 mm slabs; hand-built ocarinas use about 4.8–6.4 mm walls; the Do 3 weight suggests about 3–4 mm fired.
- Join the halves while the clay is still soft enough to bond.
- Demould as soon as the piece holds its shape. Leaving it in the mould too long makes shrinking clay fight the plaster and crack.

### Cited Findings
**Press-moulding practice (Ben Jordan) [M]** — [Ceramic Arts Network: How to Use a Plaster Press Mold](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/How-to-Use-a-Plaster-Press-Mold-to-Make-a-Cup)
- Slabs "about 1⁄8 inch thick" (≈3.2 mm).
- A metal rib compresses and smooths the slab before pressing.
- For concave forms he uses a fabric pouch filled with fine sand "to softly and evenly press the slab into the form without causing cracking or creating thin spots".
- Multi-part moulds: he scores and slips the edges, closes the mould, then pushes on the inside "pushing all of the seams together as well as ensuring that all of the surfaces are compressed".
- Demoulding: wait "about five minutes before removing the piece, just long enough to let the clay firm up enough to handle". Excessive drying in the mould risks cracks.
- The "porous plaster mold draws moisture out of the clay", with optional heat-gun acceleration.

**Wall thickness in ocarina practice**
- Elizabeth Paley (thrown ocarinas): walls "about 3/16–¼ inch (4.8–6.4 mm)" [M] — [Pottery Making Illustrated: Ocarinas](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/ocarinas)
- Forum snippets: about 8 mm in one approach, but "the thinner the better", and wall thickness at a finger hole affects airflow [S] — [Ocarina Network: Wall thickness of ocarina](https://theocarinanetwork.com/wall-thickness-of-ocarina-t18384.html) (403; snippet only)
- Rélyéf's guide: use a slab roller for even walls. "thin spots crack during drying and affect the tone". Score, slip, then press both mould halves together and trim excess with a harp cutter [M] — [Rélyéf guide](https://relyefpotterytools.com/blogs/blog-section/how-to-make-a-ceramic-ocarina-a-complete-guide-for-future-masters-of-sound)

**Budrio tradition uses plaster moulds [M]**
- In 1878 Cesare Vicinelli "ideò pure degli appositi stampi, sì che riuscì a produrre ocarine in quantità molto superiore" — [ocarina.it: La storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- The same page describes Donati's workshop with "ocarine, pinzette, stampi, blocchi di creta" — [ocarina.it: La storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- Robert Hickman's book covers "Making and using aligned plaster molds" [M] — [ocarinamaking.com](https://ocarinamaking.com/). The book's content is paywalled and was not read.

**Evenness and warping [T]**
- Even thickness matters. Stress concentrates at thin areas and sharp concave angles — [Digitalfire: Drying Ceramics Without Cracks](https://digitalfire.com/article/drying+ceramics+without+cracks)
- Leather-hard ware "is still flexible enough that pieces that have gone slightly out-of-round… can be squeezed carefully back into shape" — [Digitalfire: Leather hard](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/leather+hard)

### Inferences
**Wall thickness implied by the Do 3 weight**
- I modelled the Do 3 (215 g, 17.5 × 8.5 cm) as an ellipsoidal shell 4.5–6.5 cm deep with an *assumed* fired bulk density of 1.8–2.0 g/cm³. That gives an average wall of **≈3.1–4.0 mm fired**, and less once the mouthpiece mass is subtracted.
- The density is an unsourced assumption. Treat 3–4 mm fired (≈3.3–4.5 mm wet at 8–12 % shrinkage) as a sanity check, not a spec.
- Weighing the original and measuring its wall at a finger hole with calipers would settle it.

**Practical sequence**
- Roll or cut slabs at uniform thickness with guide sticks (e.g., 4 mm).
- Compress with a rib.
- Press into each half with a sand pouch or sponge.
- Trim flush with the mould parting face.
- Join while the clay is still plastic or just firming (section 3).
- Release after minutes to about an hour, depending on how wet the plaster is. Do not leave it overnight, because the shrinking shell can crack against the mould.

**Consistent mould moisture**
- A bone-dry plaster mould pulls water from the contact face quickly and unevenly.
- Mould moisture should be kept consistent between pressings so wall stiffness, and therefore shrinkage, is repeatable. This is my inference from Digitalfire's uneven-drying principle.

### Gaps
- I found no source giving clay moisture content (%) for press moulding terracotta, and none from a Budrio maker describing slab thickness or pressing technique.
- The Hickman book, which covers aligned plaster moulds, was not accessible.

## 3. Joining the two halves and making the seam airtight

### Takeaway
Join while both halves are plastic or soft leather-hard, at the same moisture:
- Score deeply.
- Use same-clay slip (barbotina) at thick-cream consistency, or "magic water" (sodium silicate plus soda ash).
- Apply pressure with a slight twist.
- Compress the seam inside and out.

Uneven moisture between halves, or across the seam, is the main crack driver. For repairs in dry greenware, vinegar/paper-clay slip is used, but that evidence is weaker. Test blowing before firing.

### Cited Findings
**Barry Hall's method [M]** — [Ceramic Arts Network: Making Music with Clay](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Making-Music-with-Clay-How-to-Make-a-Ceramic-Ocarina)
- "Use the serrated rib to rough up the rim of each half and paint on an even coat of water."
- "Press the halves together. A slight twisting motion will strengthen the bond."

**Hickman [M]**
- Cut the egg-shaped vessel in half at leather-hard, "heavily score the mating faces" and join with slip — [ocarinamaking.com: Xun](https://ocarinamaking.com/page/how_to_make_a_xun)

**Joining slip [T]** — [Digitalfire: Drying Ceramics Without Cracks](https://digitalfire.com/article/drying+ceramics+without+cracks)
- Slip at "thick cream consistency" with "lots of pressure".
- Too much slip creates weak points.

**Magic water recipe (Mark Arnold, *Ceramics Monthly*, Sept 2022) [T/M]** — [Ceramic Arts Network: Magic Water recipe](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/ceramic-recipes/recipe/magic-water-cfe7d44a-43e2-4e91-b04c-dfede6f8adcf)
- 1 gallon water, 3 tbsp sodium silicate, 1.5 tsp soda ash. "Mix the soda ash into warm water to dissolve, then add the sodium silicate."
- It "can be used instead of slip… requires less cleanup than slip and helps prevent cracks from forming along the joints."
- Metric equivalent (my conversion): about 3.8 L water, 44 mL sodium silicate, 7 mL soda ash.

**Magic water and vinegar mechanism [S]**
- Magic water works best at leather-hard. It is a deflocculant that lets particles at the joint intermingle — [thepotterywheel.com](https://thepotterywheel.com/magic-water-for-pottery/) (search snippet)
- Vinegar is a *flocculant*. Paper-clay slip is made by blending tissue into vinegar and then adding dry clay. The fibres limit shrinkage of the mend, and the method is used for dry or bone-dry repairs — [thepotterywheel.com: fix cracks in bone-dry clay](https://thepotterywheel.com/fix-cracks-in-bone-dry-clay/) (search snippet; exact page attribution not verified)

**Press-mould seams [M]**
- Close the mould halves, then push the seams together from inside — [Ben Jordan, CAN](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/How-to-Use-a-Plaster-Press-Mold-to-Make-a-Cup)
- Score, slip, press the two halves together, and clean the seam with a harp cutter — [Rélyéf guide](https://relyefpotterytools.com/blogs/blog-section/how-to-make-a-ceramic-ocarina-a-complete-guide-for-future-masters-of-sound)

**Drying rule applied to joins [T]**
- "At no time in the drying process should any part of a piece be significantly ahead of another part." Poor joins and thin sections drying ahead of thick ones cause cracks — [Digitalfire: Drying Ceramics Without Cracks](https://digitalfire.com/article/drying+ceramics+without+cracks)

**Test blowing [M]**
- Barry Hall and Paley's method test-blow the voicing before cutting the finger holes — [CAN Hall](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Making-Music-with-Clay-How-to-Make-a-Ceramic-Ocarina), [PMI Paley](https://pottery-making-illustrated-placeholder.invalid)

### Inferences
**Joining sequence for a two-part Budrio-style mould**
- Join while the halves are still in their moulds and still plastic, with the mould keys aligning the halves.
- Score both rims and apply slip or magic water.
- Close and clamp the moulds.
- Reinforce the inner seam through the open end or mouthpiece opening where reachable, e.g. with a bent wooden tool, as Jordan does inside his mould.
- Demould and compress the outer seam with a rib.
- The claim that Budrio makers apply the mouthpiece (bocchino) separately is only a search snippet (see Gaps). If the mouthpiece block is added separately, it is a second high-risk join.

**Blow/leak test in greenware (my own procedure)**
- Before cutting the finger holes, seal the window with a fingertip and blow into the windway. Pressure should build with no hiss at the seam.
- Also blow normally to check that the vessel sounds with all holes closed.
- After firing, a soap film on the seam, with the window blocked, can reveal a hairline leak.
- Do not wet greenware with soap solution.
- None of these is a sourced maker protocol.

**Repairs**
- Use magic water only on leather-hard joins. Keep vinegar/paper-clay slip for small dry-state repairs, and accept that such repairs in the mouthpiece area often reopen in firing.
- The reopening point is anecdotal common knowledge and unsourced.

### Gaps
- The Ocarina Network threads on seams and leaks could not be read (403).
- I found no quantitative data (e.g., leak rates, or seam-failure frequency by method) and no Budrio-specific joining description.
- The second Paley link in Cited Findings is a placeholder. Use the real Pottery Making Illustrated URL: https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/ocarinas

## 4. Windway, labium and window cutting at leather-hard: tools, sequence, anti-warp measures, edge finishing

### Takeaway
The windway is formed by an inserted flat stick or blade that stays in place as a mandrel. The window (aperture) is cut and the labium ramp (≈45°) is chiselled against that mandrel **after the body is closed**, at leather-hard, when the clay no longer drags on the knife. Keep glaze and heavy finishes off the windway and labium.

### Cited Findings
**Window and labium technique [M]**
- Paley's tools are a "needle tool and wooden craft sticks that are sanded to create beveled ends" — [PMI: Ocarinas (Elizabeth Paley)](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/ocarinas)
- She pushes a craft stick into the mouthpiece, skirting the inner cavity wall. "With the windway stick providing counter-pressure", she uses the beveled end of a second stick to cut the aperture and chisel "an approximately 45° ramp for the labium" — [PMI: Ocarinas (Elizabeth Paley)](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/ocarinas)
- Barry Hall: "Carefully insert the popsicle stick into the mouthpiece to create the windway". The stick must pass parallel to the top and bottom surfaces and square to the sides. Then "make a square cut at a 45° angle, moving toward the mouthpiece" — [CAN: Making Music with Clay](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Making-Music-with-Clay-How-to-Make-a-Ceramic-Ocarina)
- Rélyéf uses a "calibration plate" that sets the windway angle and width, then refines with a tuning or carving bit, both at leather-hard [M] — [Rélyéf guide](https://relyefpotterytools.com/blogs/blog-section/how-to-make-a-ceramic-ocarina-a-complete-guide-for-future-masters-of-sound)

**Sequence [M]**
- Hickman drills the blow hole after joining. He marks the finger holes at leather-hard and tunes them once the piece is complete and playable — [ocarinamaking.com: Xun](https://ocarinamaking.com/page/how_to_make_a_xun)
- His book covers "Functioning and creation of the windway, labium and sound hole" — [ocarinamaking.com](https://ocarinamaking.com/) (not read)

**Timing of the cut [S]**
- Wait for leather-hard before cutting the labium. Clay that is too soft drags and sticks on the knife, and the windway deforms when the stick is removed — [Ocarina Network: Ocarina Construction](https://theocarinanetwork.com/ocarina-construction-t13169.html) (403; snippet)

**Glaze exclusion [M]**
- Do not glaze "the windway" or "the beveled edge of the aperture" — [CAN Hall](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Making-Music-with-Clay-How-to-Make-a-Ceramic-Ocarina)
- "mask the windway, aperture, and all finger holes to prevent glaze" — [PMI Paley](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/ocarinas)

**Terminology [M]**
- Pure Ocarinas has a page naming the parts (windway, window/sound hole, labium) — [Pure Ocarinas: Ocarina anatomy](https://pureocarinas.com/ocarina-anatomy)

### Inferences
**Mandrel instead of a stick**
- For a repeatable Budrio copy, replace the popsicle stick with a **metal or plastic blade ("lama") sized to the fired windway plus shrinkage**, i.e. windway width and height ÷ (1 − s_total).
- Coat it with a release (talc or thin oil).
- Leave it in place, or re-insert it, while the mouthpiece firms, so the thin windway roof cannot sag.
- Pull it straight out along its axis at leather-hard.

**Protecting the windway while it dries**
- Maker sources confirm the stick is used as a mandrel. I found no source for leaving a card or feeler-gauge shim in the windway during drying. It is plausible but untested here.
- If you do it, the shim must be removed or be combustible (paper), because the clay shrinks around it. A rigid steel gauge left in could crack the roof.

**Labium edge finishing**
- Final crispness is best achieved at leather-hard with a sharp blade, then lightly refined at bone-dry with fine abrasive.
- Chipping risk is high at bone-dry, so only touch it lightly.
- The PLA master could carry the windway floor, ramp and window outline as moulded guides, to make the geometry repeatable. The cut itself still happens on the clay.

### Gaps

- No documented use of feeler gauges or cards to hold the windway during drying.
- Korean, Japanese, STL, Focalink, Mountain and Songbird maker pages were not reached. My search budget ran out, and Ocarina Network pages were blocked.

## 5. Drying schedule; pitch drift while tuning at leather-hard; when makers tune

### Takeaway
Dry **evenly rather than slowly per se**: cover with cloth plus plastic so the thin mouthpiece never runs ahead of the body, then let it reach bone-dry with no remaining coldness before firing.

Makers do most of the tuning just before or at leather-hard, deliberately flat, and finish after firing. Pitch keeps rising as the clay continues to shrink, so the tuning target must include the drying *and* firing shrinkage that remains after the moment you tune.

### Cited Findings
**Drying [T]** — [Digitalfire: Drying Ceramics Without Cracks](https://digitalfire.com/article/drying+ceramics+without+cracks)
- "it is not the speed of drying, but the unevenness of drying that results in ware either cracking."
- Recommended covering: pack ware on bats, "cover with flowing cloth… Put them inside big bags or wrap plastic around and tuck it under."
- Once moisture gradients form they persist, so prevent them from the start.
- Attachments that dry faster, such as handles (and, by analogy, the mouthpiece), should be slowed by wrapping them with wax or cloth.
- Timescales: mugs on plaster under cloth and plastic reach uniform leather-hard overnight. Large porcelain plates take about 10 days, and very plastic bodies about a month.
- For kiln drying, hold at 240 °F (≈115 °C) "for many hours (or even days)".

**Drying [M]**
- Rélyéf identifies the mouthpiece and the central seam as crack and leak points — [Rélyéf guide](https://relyefpotterytools.com/blogs/blog-section/how-to-make-a-ceramic-ocarina-a-complete-guide-for-future-masters-of-sound) (already known to the project)

**When makers tune**
- Hickman marks the holes at leather-hard and tunes once the instrument is complete and playable [M] — [ocarinamaking.com: Xun](https://ocarinamaking.com/page/how_to_make_a_xun)
- Rélyéf tunes at "leather-hard stage" with a phone tuner app [M] — [Rélyéf guide](https://relyefpotterytools.com/blogs/blog-section/how-to-make-a-ceramic-ocarina-a-complete-guide-for-future-masters-of-sound)
- **Error in the Rélyéf source:** it states "Enlarging a hole lowers the pitch". This contradicts Hall's "Enlarging a hole raises its pitch" ([CAN Hall](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Making-Music-with-Clay-How-to-Make-a-Ceramic-Ocarina)) and Helmholtz physics (section 7).
- Hall: "cut and tune one hole at a time", starting small and enlarging [M] — [CAN Hall](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Making-Music-with-Clay-How-to-Make-a-Ceramic-Ocarina)
- Forum practice [S] — [Ocarina Network: Tuning an occarina](https://theocarinanetwork.com/tuning-an-occarina-t22633.html) and [Making an Ocarina?](https://theocarinanetwork.com/making-an-ocarina-t24327.html) (403; snippets):
  - "Most makers do major tuning a little before the leather hard state."
  - Some tune just before leather-hard with the tuner set to **A415 Hz** instead of 440 Hz, to allow for the rise in drying and firing.
  - Makers usually tune mainly wet, then make small corrections after firing with a Dremel.
- Budrio claim [S]: holes are made and initial tuning is done at leather-hard, "perfected after firing". I saw this only in a search summary attributed to ocarina.it/Menaglio and could not find it on the ocarina.it pages I fetched (see Gaps).

**Leather-hard moisture [T]**
- Leather-hard is about 15 % moisture — [Wikipedia: Leather-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather-hard)

### Inferences
**Pitch drift during a tuning session**
- A leather-hard ocarina keeps losing water while you tune. Each minute of work shrinks it slightly, so the note creeps sharp.
- Tuning the first holes and re-checking them at the end of the session will show the drift. Work under a damp cloth between steps.
- Tuner readings on damp clay are also affected by your breath temperature and by wet, soft labium edges. Tune at a consistent, moderate breath pressure.
- The A415 practice builds in ≈101 cents (1200·log₂(440/415)) of pre-flatness. That is right only for a body whose remaining shrinkage from the tuning point is about 5.5–6 % (see section 7). A low-shrinkage terracotta needs much less.

**Recommended tuning order** (combining sources)
1. Cut the voicing and get a clean fundamental with all holes closed.
2. Tune the finger holes from small to large, slightly flat.
3. Dry evenly under cover.
4. Fire.
5. Finish-tune by enlarging holes (section 9).

### Gaps
- I found no maker or engineering data measuring pitch (cents) against moisture during drying, and no measurement of how much linear shrinkage remains after "leather-hard".
- Both must be measured with the test-bar protocol in section 1, or with a pilot ocarina tuned and re-measured at each stage.

## 6. Firing: schedule for small hollow vessels, peak, soak, cooling; bisque vs glaze; atmosphere; smoke/pit/raku; explosion risk

### Takeaway
A single low firing is the norm for plain terracotta ocarinas: bisque only, cone 06–04, about 1000–1060 °C, or 900–1000 °C in Brazilian and Budrio practice. Pure Ocarinas notes a later refire at 600–800 °C "won't affect the tuning".

The critical risk is residual water. Explosion is caused by steam, not air.
- Water-smoke hold at about 100–120 °C until no moisture appears at the peephole.
- Go moderately up to about 1000 °F / 540 °C.
- Ease through 573 °C.
- Slow the last 50–60 °C to peak with a short soak.
- Cool naturally.
- Keep oxidation, with ventilation, for a red colour and no black core.

### Cited Findings
**Plainsman BQ1000 electric bisque schedule [T]** — [Digitalfire: BQ1000](https://digitalfire.com/schedule/bq1000)

| Step | Rate | Target | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 170 °C/h | 120 °C | 60 min |
| 2 | 170 °C/h | 945 °C | — |
| 3 | 60 °C/h | 1000 °C | 15 min |
| 4 | free-fall cooling | — | — |

- Step 1: "Hold… as long as you need to… This could be an hour for light ware or ten hours for heavy ware."
- Ventilation during the climb prevents black coring from iron reduction.
- Free-fall cooling is fine because "bisque ware is porous".

**Cone 04 bisque programme [M/T]** — [KilnSchedule: Bisque firing schedule](https://kilnschedule.com/bisque-firing-schedule/)
- 80 °F/h to 250 °F (candle/water-smoke).
- 200 °F/h to 1000 °F.
- **100 °F/h from 1000 to 1100 °F** (quartz inversion zone).
- 180 °F/h to 1695 °F.
- 80 °F/h to about 1940 °F.
- Bisque runs between cone 08 and 04, "roughly 1728 to 1940 F (942 to 1060 C)".
- "Go slow, especially on thick or damp ware, or trapped steam cracks the piece."

**Quartz inversion [T]** — [Digitalfire: Quartz inversion](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/quartz+inversion)
- The sudden volume change is centred on 573 °C.
- During heat-up, porous ware has "plenty of pore space to absorb the change". Cooling, and dense or refired ware, are the riskier cases.
- The page mentions very slow rates (e.g., 25 degrees/hour) through the range for at-risk ware.

**Explosions (Sue McLeod) [T]** — [Ceramics Monthly Techno File: Air Bubble Myth](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/ceramics-monthly/ceramics-monthly-article/Techno-File-Air-Bubble-Myth)
- Explosions come from water turning to steam, which "expands greatly in size—over 1500 times". Air pockets only matter because they trap moisture.
- "If the clay is 100% dry inside and out at the time it reaches boiling temperature, there is zero risk of explosion."
- Check for moisture during candling by holding glass or plastic at a peephole until no condensation appears.
- Closed hollow forms need a vent hole [S] — [ClayGeek](https://www.claygeek.com/ceramics-101/5-ways-to-prevent-explosions-in-your-kiln/) (snippet)

**Refiring and tuning [M]**
- Refiring an earthenware ocarina at "600 to 800 °C (1112 to 1472 °F) is adequate and won't affect the tuning" — [Pure Ocarinas](https://pureocarinas.com/ocarina-materials-finish-differences)

**Other firing types**
- Stoneware reference: Paley fires her ocarinas "in oxidation to cone 6" (stoneware). Firing raises the fundamental while "melodic intervals generated by finger holes are generally preserved" [M] — [PMI Paley](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/ocarinas)
- Raku: fired at about 900–1000 °C in about 1 h, with the red-hot piece reduced in combustibles. Crackle darkened by smoke is typical [M] — [Tauá Cerâmica](https://www.tauaceramica.com/post/2016/02/24/os-tipos-de-queima)
- Terra sigillata surfaces are commonly pit- or saggar-fired: "a canvas for the fire to paint on" [M] — [CAN Terra Sigillata 101, Sumi von Dassow](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Terra-Sigillata-101-How-to-Make-Apply-and-Troubleshoot-Terra-Sig)

**Colour and overfiring [T]**
- Terracotta darkens with temperature. "Accidental overfiring drastically darkens the color" — [Digitalfire: Terra Cotta](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/terra%20cotta)

**Budrio firing history [M]**
- Donati's worry when moving house was finding "un altro forno per cuocervi le ocarine". Vicinelli worked at the "Fornace Silvani", a brick and tile works — [ocarina.it: La storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)

### Inferences
**Suggested programme for a rented studio kiln** (my synthesis of BQ1000, KilnSchedule and McLeod; not a published ocarina schedule)
1. 50–80 °C/h to 110–120 °C, then hold at least 2 h. Longer if the load is damp, until the peephole mirror test is clean.
2. 100–170 °C/h to 540 °C.
3. About 55 °C/h (100 °F/h) from 540 to 600 °C.
4. 150–170 °C/h to about 60 °C below peak.
5. 60 °C/h to the **same peak as the test bars**, e.g. cone 05–04, ≈1000–1060 °C, or the studio's standard biscoito. Soak 10–15 min.
6. Natural cooling. Do not open above about 100–150 °C.

**Venting**
- An ocarina is vented through the windway, window and finger holes, so the "closed form" explosion risk is lower than for a sealed sculpture.
- The thick mouthpiece block and the seam dry last, so they are where trapped moisture sits. Dry at least 1–2 weeks in Goiânia's dry season, longer in the rainy season. This timescale is an inference; I found no source for it.

**Atmosphere**
- An electric studio kiln is oxidising. Keep the vents or bungs open early for a clean red colour.
- Smoke, pit or raku effects add thermal-shock risk to a 3–4 mm thin-walled instrument, and carbon adds no sealing.
- If you want those effects, do a normal bisque first and then a low smoke firing below the bisque temperature. That way the tuning is not changed; Pure Ocarinas' 600–800 °C refire note supports this.

**Glaze firing**
- If you glaze, fire the glaze at or below the bisque temperature. A higher second firing adds shrinkage and pushes the pitch sharp after tuning. This is inferred from the shrinkage data plus the Pure Ocarinas refire note.

### Gaps
- The Budrio firing temperature ("900–1000 °C, gradual heating and equally gradual cooling") appeared only in a search-engine summary attributed to ocarina.it/Fabio Menaglio. It was **not found** on the ocarina.it pages I fetched (home, acquista, metodo, negozio, storia). Treat it as unverified.
- I found no published firing schedule written specifically for ocarinas, and no Orton cone table verified from Orton's own site (their URLs 404'd).
- The cone temperatures above come from Digitalfire and KilnSchedule and depend on heating rate.

## 7. Pitch change in firing: quantitative data, prediction and compensation

### Takeaway
Makers report rises from drying and firing of anything from **≈5 cents** (Hickman), through **≈15–20 cents**, up to **about a semitone**, because shrinkage differs by clay and by when tuning happened.

Physics gives a simple predictor. For a uniformly scaled Helmholtz resonator, frequency ∝ 1/linear size. So the rise in cents is 1200·log₂(1/(1 − r)), where r is the linear shrinkage remaining after you tune. That is **≈17.4 cents per 1 % of remaining shrinkage**, and intervals are preserved.

### Cited Findings
**Physics [T]** — [Wikipedia: Helmholtz resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz_resonance)
- f = (v/2π)·√(A/(V₀·L_eq)).
- "An ocarina is essentially a Helmholtz resonator where the combined area of the opened finger holes determines the note."

**Maker reports [S]** — [Ocarina Network: Tuning an occarina](https://theocarinanetwork.com/tuning-an-occarina-t22633.html) (403; snippets)
- "the pitch will raise usually significantly by the end of the firing although this depends on the shrinkage of your clay."
- Robert Hickman reported a rise of only **5 cents**. Others report about **15–20 cents**.
- For mid-range ocarinas (alto C), shrinkage brings the pitch up about **a half-step**. Tune about a half-step flat.
- Makers aim for every ocarina to come out of the kiln slightly flat.
- The **A415** tuner practice is about a semitone flat (see section 5).

**Direction confirmed [M]**
- Paley: "Firing raises an ocarina's fundamental tone", and intervals are "generally preserved" — [PMI Paley](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/ocarinas)

**Shrinkage ranges that feed the model [T]**
- Earthenware firing shrinkage is 3–4 % or less, sometimes about 0 or negative — [Digitalfire: Firing Shrinkage](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/firing+shrinkage)
- Red ceramics: firing shrinkage below 3 %, drying shrinkage 3–10 % — [Zaccaron et al. 2016](https://periodicos.utfpr.edu.br/recc/article/download/6631/4278)

### Inferences
**Scaling law**
- Shrink every length by a factor k. Then A ∝ k², V ∝ k³ and L_eq ∝ k (wall thickness and the equivalent length of the holes scale too).
- So f ∝ √(k²/(k³·k)) = 1/k.
- A uniform linear shrinkage r raises *every* note by the same ratio 1/(1 − r). This explains Paley's "intervals preserved".

**Look-up table** (my arithmetic)

| Remaining linear shrinkage r | Pitch rise |
|---|---|
| 0.3 % | +5 cents (≈ Hickman's report) |
| 0.5 % | +9 cents |
| 1 % | +17 cents (≈ the 15–20 cent reports) |
| 2 % | +35 cents |
| 3 % | +53 cents |
| 4 % | +71 cents |
| 5 % | +89 cents |
| 5.7 % | +101 cents (= A415 practice ≈ semitone) |
| 6 % | +107 cents |
| 8 % | +144 cents |

**Reconciling the maker reports**
- 5–20 cents fits makers who tune near bone-dry, or clays with low firing shrinkage: only firing shrinkage of about 0.3–1.2 % remains.
- A semitone fits tuning while quite wet, with about 5–6 % of drying plus firing shrinkage still to come.

**Compensation recipe**
- Measure r from tuning point to fired with the test bars.
- Tune target = final pitch − 1200·log₂(1/(1 − r)) cents. For example, with r = 3 %, tune about 50 cents flat.
- Add a small safety margin flat (e.g., 10–20 cents) because post-firing correction can only raise pitch.

**Master scale error has the same effect**
- If the upper-bound shrinkage assumed for the master exceeds the real shrinkage, the fired body is larger than target. The whole instrument is then flat by 1200·log₂((1 − s_real)/(1 − s_assumed)).
- Example: assuming 12 % when the real value is 8 % → ≈77 cents flat.
- The **lowest note (all holes closed) depends on cavity volume and the window/voicing area**, not on the finger holes. Finger holes can be enlarged to correct each note, but a flat fundamental can only be raised by enlarging the window/voicing, which changes tone and breath response.
- So "flat is fixable" is true for finger-hole notes but only partly true for the bottom note.
- Mitigation: slightly under-size the window in the master and open it at leather-hard while tuning the fundamental.

**Non-uniform effects** (not captured by the scaling law)
- Anisotropic shrinkage in pressed parts, seam compression, warping, and changes in windway height or labium position in the kiln can shift the voicing and individual notes by a few cents.
- Expect a residual per-note scatter even with a perfect model.

### Gaps
- I found no published controlled measurement of cents change against measured shrinkage for ocarinas. All maker figures are anecdotal and came from snippets of blocked forum pages.
- No figure specific to Budrio makers was found.

## 8. Post-firing finishing and sealing: effect on tone, tuning and breath moisture; Budrio practice; mouth-contact

### Takeaway
Plain porous earthenware is acoustically and ergonomically good: it soaks up condensation and gives dry, slidable fingers. It discolours over time from skin oils.

Budrio instruments traditionally carry a **brown varnish over red terracotta**. Mignani's are described as "verniciata in colore bruno" and "not completely opaque". Menaglio also offers a glazed ("ceramicata") version.

Any finish should stay out of the windway and off the labium edge. Because a coating inside a hole narrows it slightly and lowers pitch, do the final tuning *after* applying the finish or re-check afterwards.

### Cited Findings
**Pure Ocarinas on materials and finishes [M]** — [Pure Ocarinas: materials, finish differences and care](https://pureocarinas.com/ocarina-materials-finish-differences)
- "Earthenware ocarinas never suffer from condensation or moisture build up as condensation soaks into the porosity of the material."
- Finishes include "fired glazes, natural shellac, and plain ceramic". "Synthetic finishes including lacquers and epoxy are also becoming more common."
- "Fired glazes and synthetic finishes are the most durable. Shellac is a middle ground, it is pretty soft, but has other ergonomic advantages."
- Mirror-smooth gloss makes sliding to half-holed notes difficult. Plain ceramic "absorbs any moisture on the fingers".
- "Plain earthenware naturally discolours over time as it absorbs skin oils into its pores."
- Never use alcohol-based cleaners on shellac finishes. Avoid scented cleaners, which porous earthenware absorbs.

**Budrio and Mignani finishes [M]**
- Mignani sopranino: "in terracotta, verniciata in colore bruno" — [Fondazione Carisbo](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/ocarina-sopranino-arrigo-mignani-c-1985)
- Catalogue description of a Mignani set [S]: surface covered in a dark-brown varnish, "not completely opaque", reddish Bologna terracotta, 10 holes (8 front, 2 back). Instruments marked "M" were his "ocarine scelte" — [Catalogo Patrimonio Emilia-Romagna](https://bbcc.regione.emilia-romagna.it/pater/loadcard.do?id_card=71029) (403; snippet)
- Menaglio lists "Ocarina sol2 in terracotta ceramicata" (glazed terracotta) alongside plain terracotta — [ocarina.it](https://www.ocarina.it/)

**Glaze masking [M]**
- Don't glaze the windway or aperture bevel — [CAN Hall](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Making-Music-with-Clay-How-to-Make-a-Ceramic-Ocarina)
- Mask the windway, aperture and finger holes. Patinas, slips or underglazes are alternatives — [PMI Paley](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/pottery-making-illustrated/pottery-making-illustrated-article/ocarinas)

**Glaze fit on terracotta [T]**
- A well-fitted glaze makes terra cotta serviceable. If the glaze crazes, the craze lines "enable water to penetrate and absorb into the porous clay matrix" — [Digitalfire: Terra Cotta](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/terra%20cotta)

**Shellac [T]** — [Wikipedia: Shellac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellac)
- Edible. Used as "confectioner's glaze", food additive E904.
- Dissolved in alcohol for application.
- Melting point 75 °C. "scratches more easily than most lacquers".
- "For water-resistance and durability, it does not keep up with synthetic finishing products."
- Used to French-polish fine string instruments.

**Terra sigillata (Sumi von Dassow) [M]** — [CAN Terra Sigillata 101](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Terra-Sigillata-101-How-to-Make-Apply-and-Troubleshoot-Terra-Sig)
- Apply to bone-dry or nearly bone-dry ware.
- Specific gravity 1.15.
- At least three thin coats, buffed when the surface "looks waxy and grey but is no longer wet-looking".
- Often used for pit or saggar firing.

**Burnishing [T]**
- Done on a leather-hard vessel by rubbing with a polished stone or glass — [Wikipedia: Leather-hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather-hard)

**Coatings inside holes [S]**
- Nail polish applied inside holes has been suggested to nudge the pitch back down after over-enlarging — [Ocarina Network: How to raise the tuning…](https://theocarinanetwork.com/how-to-raise-the-tuning-of-an-ocarina-to-make-it-q-t24255.html) (403; snippet)

### Inferences
**Budrio-style finish for this project**
- Leave the interior **unsealed**. This keeps the condensation benefit Pure Ocarinas describes and does not narrow the windway.
- On the outside, choose a thin brown-tinted finish that seals against skin oils. Options:
  - shellac (edible, E904; soft, and alcohol-damaged);
  - a hard oil or wax;
  - a food-contact-rated clear varnish.
- Keep all finishes off the windway, labium and hole walls, or apply them there only before the final tuning pass.

**Terra sigillata alternative**
- Terra sig plus burnishing on the greenware gives a natural sheen with no organic coating. Fire it at or below cone 04, and the surface remains somewhat porous.
- Terra sig must be applied at bone-dry, *after* the leather-hard tuning, so it must not be brushed into holes or the windway.

**Glazed outside**
- Use a commercial **lead-free** low-fire glaze fitted to the body, and mask the whole voicing area.
- The glaze firing must not exceed the bisque temperature (section 6).

**Effect on tuning**
- Any film in the finger holes reduces the hole area A. From the Helmholtz formula, even 0.1–0.2 mm of coating in a 6–10 mm hole lowers the note by several cents.
- This is arithmetic inference; I have no measured data. Re-check the tuning after finishing.

### Gaps
- I found no source naming the exact varnish (resin or solvent) that Mignani or current Budrio makers use.
- I found no measured data on how any finish changes tone or cents.
- Mouth-contact and food-safety aspects are left to the health/safety researcher. I noted only shellac's edible status and the lead-free glaze principle.

## 9. Post-firing tuning: tools, adjustment range, lowering pitch

### Takeaway
After firing, tuning is **subtractive**. Enlarge or undercut the finger holes, and if necessary the window, with diamond burrs in a rotary tool, while wearing a mask. This only raises pitch, which is why every maker aims to come out of the kiln slightly flat.

Lowering pitch after firing is limited to making a hole smaller:
- a coating inside the hole (e.g., nail polish, or by inference wax);
- a filler, e.g. epoxy, which is not sourced.

These are cosmetic and durability compromises.

### Cited Findings
**Tools and practice [S]** — [Ocarina Network: Tuning an occarina](https://theocarinanetwork.com/tuning-an-occarina-t22633.html), [Little Geese forum: Dremel tuning](http://www.littlegeese.com/viewtopic.php?f=9&t=649) (both inaccessible; snippets)
- Diamond burrs are preferred over abrasive stones, which "wear, break, or crumble more quickly and leave more discoloration on fired clay".
- Maker Jade Everett tunes after firing with a Dremel while wearing a dust mask.
- Makers intend kiln output to be slightly flat so they can "file off material from some tone holes".

**Raising pitch [T/M]**
- Enlarging a hole raises its pitch — [CAN Hall](https://ceramicartsnetwork.org/daily/article/Making-Music-with-Clay-How-to-Make-a-Ceramic-Ocarina)
- Consistent with f ∝ √A — [Wikipedia: Helmholtz resonance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmholtz_resonance)

**Lowering pitch [S]**
- Nail polish applied inside the holes — [Ocarina Network thread t24255](https://theocarinanetwork.com/how-to-raise-the-tuning-of-an-ocarina-to-make-it-q-t24255.html) (snippet)

**Refire tolerance [M]**
- Refiring at 600–800 °C does not affect tuning — [Pure Ocarinas](https://pureocarinas.com/ocarina-materials-finish-differences)
- This means dust or oil from post-firing tuning could be burned off without re-tuning (inference).

### Inferences
**How much can be adjusted**
- Near the operating point, f ∝ √A. So enlarging a hole's effective area by x % raises that note's contribution by roughly x/2 % in frequency.
- Example: +10 % area gives about +5 % contribution, i.e. tens of cents, on that note.
- But each hole acts together with the others and with the window. The practical limit is ergonomic, since finger-covering size limits holes, and tonal, since oversized holes weaken high notes.
- This is not quantified in the sources. Work in small passes and re-check the whole scale after each pass.

**Undercutting**
- Chamfering or undercutting the inner edge of a hole, rather than widening the outer rim, raises pitch while keeping the outer diameter coverable.
- This is consistent with the forum point that wall thickness at the hole affects airflow ([Ocarina Network t18384](https://theocarinanetwork.com/wall-thickness-of-ocarina-t18384.html), snippet). The technique itself is not sourced here.

**Safety note**
- Dry-grinding fired clay produces silica dust. Wet-grind or use extraction and a mask.
- Details are left to the safety researcher.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative data (cents per mm of enlargement) from makers.
- No Budrio-specific post-firing tuning description was found. The "perfected after firing" claim is snippet-only.
- Budget exhaustion (search limit reached) and 403 blocks on the Ocarina Network, the Emilia-Romagna catalogue and Little Geese prevented deeper verification of forum-sourced figures.
