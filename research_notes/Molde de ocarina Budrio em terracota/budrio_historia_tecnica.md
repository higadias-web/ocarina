# Budrio ocarina: history, lineage and traditional construction (focus: Mignani / Menaglio "Do 3", C5–F6)

Source-type tags: **[P]** primary/official (maker's own site, municipal/De.Co. documents, museum catalogue); **[S]** secondary (press, encyclopaedias, reviewers, collectors' essays); **[D]** dealer/commercial listing; **[F]** forum anecdote (The Ocarina Network; pages blocked by Cloudflare during this research, so only search-engine extracts were read, and exact thread attribution is uncertain). Inferences are kept in separate sections. Research date: 2026-09-28.

## 1. Full lineage, dates, mould ownership, and the Gruppo Ocarinistico Budriese (GOB)

### Takeaway
The lineage is well documented: Donati (1853) → Mezzetti brothers (Paris/London) → Cesare Vicinelli (from 1878; devised the moulds, "Stradivari of the ocarina") → Guido Chiesa (inherited Vicinelli's workshop, tools and press in 1920) and Emilio Cesari (Vicinelli's pupil) → Arrigo Mignani (1963/64–1992, **used Cesari's moulds**, not Vicinelli/Chiesa's) → Fabio Menaglio (from 1989, the only current maker in Budrio). Menaglio's official biography says he **reconstructed Vicinelli's form by analysis**. It does **not** say he copied Vicinelli's moulds or press; that claim comes only from a forum post. Nobody publishes where the Cesari/Mignani moulds are today.

### Cited Findings
**Giuseppe Donati (inventor)**
- Born in Budrio on 2 Dec 1836. He played clarinet in the Budrio band and organ in churches. He invented the ocarina at 17 in 1853, starting from a clay "cornetta" that broke, and bored "buchi per tutte le dieci dita in posizioni comode" (holes for all ten fingers) [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- Dates given as (Budrio 1836 – Milan 1925). He organised the first quintet in 1863, which became a sextet "l'anno successivo" (p.1) or in 1865 (p.2); the document contradicts itself [P] — [De.Co. Disciplinare PDF](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf)
- His workshops were in Budrio, then Bologna, then Milan. In the 1909 interview (Otello Cavara, *Ars et Labor* LXIV/7, 1909, reprinted on Menaglio's site), the elderly Donati worries about finding "un altro forno per cuocervi le ocarine" (another kiln to fire them). He is described as surrounded by "ocarine, pinzette, stampi, blocchi di creta" (ocarinas, tweezers, **moulds**, blocks of clay) [P, reprint] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- The museum holds early Donati ocarinas from the mid-1800s made "manualmente e senza l'uso di stampi" (by hand, without moulds) [P] — [Comune di Budrio, museum page](https://www.comune.budrio.bo.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/museo-dellocarina-e-degli-strumenti-in-terracotta)
- Folklore conflicts: one source calls Donati "a teenage brick maker" [S] ([Folk Flute World](https://folkfluteworld.com/reviews/review_of_fabio_ocarina/review_of_fabio_ocarina.html)). Another says he was "a teenage baker's apprentice" who fired prototypes in the bread oven [S] ([Campin](http://www.campin.me.uk/Music/Ocarina/)). The Italian primary texts describe him only as a musician.
- He published *Metodo facile per imparare l'ocarina italiana pubblicato dall'inventore*, Fratelli Cocchi, Bologna, 1893 [P, bibliography] — [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf)

**Ercole (1841–1918) and Alberto Napoleone (1843–1906) Mezzetti**
- They made ocarinas in Paris from 1870 [P] ([ocarina.it](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)), or "verso il 1878" [P] ([Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf)). They had a London branch, A.N. Mezzetti patented a piston ("pompa o pistone") ocarina and published a method, and their materials later went to their collaborator Ercole Roda [P] (Disciplinare).
- The museum says Alberto Mezzetti was a maker "dal 1870 al 1912", with Liberty (Art Nouveau) friezes and a "stantuffo metallico" (metal plunger) [P] — [Comune museum page](https://www.comune.budrio.bo.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/museo-dellocarina-e-degli-strumenti-in-terracotta). This conflicts with A.N. Mezzetti's death in 1906 (Disciplinare).

**Cesare Vicinelli (1841–1920)**
- He began making ocarinas in 1878 at "Fornace Silvani" in Budrio, after Donati had moved to Bologna. He was the son of a fornaciaio (kiln worker) and an expert fornaciaio himself, and also played guitar, trombone and bombardino. "ideò pure degli appositi stampi" (he also devised special moulds), which let him produce far more than Donati. He died in March 1920 [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html); same facts in the [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf) with dates 1841–1920.
- He "lavorava circondato di segreti" (worked surrounded by secrets), so Chiesa "non aveva imparato molto" from him [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- "Nel 1878 il Vicinelli … inventerà gli stampi per la produzione in serie" (in 1878 Vicinelli invented the moulds for series production) [S] — [Biblioteca Salaborsa, Cronologia](https://www.bibliotecasalaborsa.it/bolognaonline/events/locarina_di_budrio)
- Vicinelli "inventò i primi stampi di metallo" (invented the first **metal** moulds). The same article calls him part of Donati's original quintet [S, journalism 2026] — [Il Post, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/04/17/ocarina-budrio-musica-festival/). The quintet claim is **contradicted** by the Disciplinare, whose first quintet is Donati, E. and A.N. Mezzetti, Ulisse Avoni and Federico Vignoli; Vicinelli led the first *settimino* in 1873.
- A caption on Menaglio's site reads: "Bilanciere per la costruzione delle ocarine realizzato da Cesare Vicinelli che lo utilizzò fin dalla fine dell'800 e lo lasciò in eredità a Guido Chiesa che lo usò fino al 1964" (a screw/fly press built by Vicinelli, used from the late 1800s, inherited by Chiesa, who used it until 1964) [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)

**Guido Chiesa**
- Born 1884. He had helped Vicinelli for 24 years and inherited his workshop, house, furniture and all tools in 1920. He made 20–30 ocarinas per day [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- Born in Budrio on 11 Aug 1884. He took over Vicinelli's workshop in 1920 and early on supplied instruments to Donati himself [P, museum catalogue] — [Carisbo, Tagliavini L6-8](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/tre-ocarine-guido-chiesa-c-1955)
- **Conflict:** the Disciplinare gives Chiesa as **(1896–1963)**, working "per ben 44 anni" with Vicinelli's inherited tools [P] ([Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf)). This disagrees with the 1884 birth year (Carisbo, ocarina.it) and with his use of the press "until 1964" (ocarina.it).

**Emilio Cesari**
- Born in Budrio on 28 Dec 1888. He was a horn player. He made ocarinas at Le Crete (Budrio) from 1920 to 1927 and directed the GOB from 1925 to 1927. He moved to Sanremo (casino orchestra) and resumed making in 1940. He died in Sanremo on 30 Mar 1962. His inventions include the "ocarinton" (tuning plugs), the "d'armonia" ocarina, the two-octave "flautino", the "doppia" (derived from Luigi Silvestri) and the "tripla" [P] — [Carisbo L1-3](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/trio-d-ocarine-emilio-cesari-c-1954)
- Cesari was Vicinelli's "allievo" (pupil) and made ocarinas in Sanremo until 1962 [P] — [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf). He worked in Vicinelli's workshop while still a Conservatory student [P] — [ocarina.it](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)

**Arrigo Mignani (1918–1997)**
- Born in Budrio on 10 Sep 1918. Violinist and member of the "Concerto d'ocarine". Began making ocarinas in 1963. Died in Budrio on 2 Nov 1997 and was posthumously awarded the Premio Città di Budrio [P] — [Carisbo L10](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/ocarina-sopranino-arrigo-mignani-c-1985)
- He joined the ocarina concert in 1963. He asked Chiesa to sell him the business and teach him; Chiesa wanted 20 million lire. Cesari's heirs sold him the "attrezzatura, ormai inutilizzata" (the unused equipment of the Sanremo maker) for 1 million lire. His first complete concert set came in 1964, and he continued "per ben ventotto anni" (for 28 years) [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html); the same text appears at [Ocarina Ensemble Budrio](https://www.ocarinaensemble.it/ocarina-di-budrio/)
- Mignani "si avvalse degli stampi già utilizzati da Emilio Cesari" (used the moulds previously used by Cesari) and was "componente-capogruppo" (member and leader) of the group [P] — [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf)
- According to a dealer: originally a stonemason (tombstones) like his father; business closed in 1992; 10,000–12,000 ocarinas per year at peak; his Do 6 and Do 7 were made "entirely by hand rather than using a mould"; stamp "DITTA A. MIGNANI BUDRIO" [D] — [VM Collectables](https://vmcollectables.com/product/contrabass-ocarina-mignani/)
- Forum claim: "Mignani bought the workshop from Chiesa's family" [F] — [The Ocarina Network](https://theocarinanetwork.com/a-good-budrio-style-ocarina-t7918.html) (search extract). This is **contradicted** by ocarina.it and the Disciplinare (he bought Cesari's equipment).

**Fabio Menaglio (b. 2 Dec 1967)**
- Trained at a vocational school for industry and crafts, then worked as a mechanical draughtsman and programmer. When he learned that Mignani "avrebbe chiuso i battenti praticamente senza lasciare eredi" (would close practically without heirs), he took up the craft. He "inizia ad analizzare e comparare il lavoro di Vicinelli fino all'essenza pura" (began analysing and comparing Vicinelli's work down to its pure essence). "la sua storia non è di un apprendistato da bottega" (his story is not a workshop apprenticeship). GOB tested his "Ocarine da Concerto" prototypes and adopted them. He has worked since 1989, and Japan was his first foreign market [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- Workshop at Via Vittime delle Foibe 6, 40054 Budrio; tel. 051 800958; info2010@ocarina.it [P] — [Turismo in Pianura](https://turismoinpianura.cittametropolitana.bo.it/it/luoghi/luoghi-di-shopping/ocarina-di-budrio)
- The Disciplinare credits the high quality of Menaglio's ocarinas "sin dai primi anni '90" (since the early 1990s) with raising the GOB's level, and calls him "l'attuale erede" (the current heir) [P] — [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf)
- Forum claim: "Fabio Menaglio copied the molds and the press of Vicinelli (which are now in the museum in Budrio)", and "molds used for Menaglio's ocarinas are copies of Vicinelli's molds" [F] — [The Ocarina Network](https://theocarinanetwork.com/a-good-budrio-style-ocarina-t7918.html) (search extract)

**Other makers**
- Italian makers of the Italian type listed today: Benvenuto Fecchio and Claudio Colombo [S] ([Ocarina Rave](https://www.ocarinarave.it/costruttori-di-ocarina/fabio-menaglio/)). Giorgio Pacchioni makes Italian ocarinas **in Brazil** [S] ([Campin](http://www.campin.me.uk/Music/Ocarina/)).

**Gruppo Ocarinistico Budriese (GOB)**
- The first written mention is a 1865 article in *La Marmitta* about a lawsuit over nighttime noise. The Bologna debut was at Teatro Brunetti (now Teatro Duse) on 16 Nov 1869. In 1870 the group split and Grossi re-formed it as a sextet, "I Celebri Montanari degli Appennini". The seventh ocarina (the bass) was added in 1873. Tours: Vienna 1873, Paris and London 1874, Madrid and Lisbon 1876; a split into two groups in 1876. Mostra Etnografica in Rome, 1911 (Testi). Directed by Barattoni from 1926. Columbia recordings in Milan, 1929. The group appeared in Bertolucci's *Novecento* (1976) and dissolved in 1977. The Scuola di ocarina opened in 1978. The current director is Emiliano Bernagozzi [S] — [Wikipedia IT](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruppo_ocarinistico_budriese)
- Per the Disciplinare: the 1873 settimino was led by Vicinelli; Barattoni directed 1926–1948; Ettore Spiga led until the mid-1970s; Paolo Scazzieri, the first teacher of the 1978 school, formed a settimino in 1979 from which today's GOB descends directly. Tours to Japan in 2014, 2015, 2018, 2019 and 2023 [P] — [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf). The Disciplinare's discography dates the Columbia 78s (D18362–D18366) to 1930, against 1929 in Wikipedia.

### Inferences
- The mould lineage probably has two branches. The **Vicinelli → Chiesa** branch includes the original press and workshop; Chiesa died c.1963/64, and the tools may have reached the museum through the Barnaba collection. The **Vicinelli-pupil → Cesari (Sanremo) → Mignani** branch is the one Mignani used. So a "Mignani Do 3" descends from Cesari's moulds, not directly from Vicinelli's.
- Menaglio consciously departed from Mignani's shapes. He criticises "due generazioni di costruttori di minore sensibilità" (two generations of less sensitive makers), which would include Chiesa/Cesari and Mignani. Mignani and Menaglio Do 3 geometries therefore probably differ.
- "Mignani closed without heirs" suggests the Cesari/Mignani moulds did not pass to Menaglio. Their current owner is unknown.

### Gaps
- Current whereabouts of the Cesari/Mignani moulds and of Mignani's workshop. No source found.
- The official museum inventory of the Vicinelli press ("bilanciere") and moulds could not be confirmed. The claim rests on a forum post plus a photo caption on ocarina.it.
- Mignani's workshop address was not found. The Catawiki listing ([link](https://www.catawiki.com/en/l/37807359-arrigo-mignani-ocarina-in-fa-budrio-ocarina-in-fa-italy-1968)) was blocked (Access Denied).

## 2. Traditional technique: moulds, press, halves, windway/labium, drying, firing, tuning, finish

### Takeaway
Hard technical data (firing temperature, tuning procedure, windway tools) is **not published** in any source found. What is documented:
- Vicinelli was a kiln man who devised moulds (described as metal in one 2026 press source).
- Production used a screw/fly press ("bilanciere") from the late 1800s until 1964.
- Donati himself had moulds by 1909 and fired in his own kiln.
- Historic instruments were terracotta "verniciata in colore bruno" (coated brown), with maker stamps on the mouthpiece.
- Giorgio Pacchioni, a traditional-style maker and researcher, states that the Italian tradition **never used plaster moulds**, only wooden or metal forms and later presses. The project's plaster press-mould approach is therefore a modern (valid) deviation.

### Cited Findings
- "the Italian tradition never used plaster casting molds, but only wooden or metal cones and (later, presses)". Pacchioni studied "manual working tools of the first Budrio builders, before the aid of presses" [S, maker interview] — [Pure Ocarinas, Pacchioni interview](https://pureocarinas.com/intervista-giorgio-pacchioni)
- Vicinelli "inventò i primi stampi di metallo" (invented the first metal moulds) [S] — [Il Post 2026](https://www.ilpost.it/2026/04/17/ocarina-budrio-musica-festival/)
- The Vicinelli **bilanciere** (screw/fly press) was used from the late 1800s, then by Chiesa until 1964 [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- Donati's 1909 workshop contained "pinzette, stampi, blocchi di creta" (tweezers, moulds, clay blocks) and his own kiln ("forno per cuocervi le ocarine") [P reprint] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- The earliest Donati instruments were made by hand without moulds [P] — [Comune museum page](https://www.comune.budrio.bo.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/museo-dellocarina-e-degli-strumenti-in-terracotta). Mignani's Do 6 and Do 7 were hand-built rather than moulded [D] — [VM Collectables](https://vmcollectables.com/product/contrabass-ocarina-mignani/)
- Production rates: Chiesa made 20–30 per day [P] ([ocarina.it](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)); Mignani 10,000–12,000 per year at peak [D] ([VM Collectables](https://vmcollectables.com/product/contrabass-ocarina-mignani/))
- **Finish:** Mignani L10 is "in terracotta, verniciata in colore bruno", stamped on the mouthpiece "DITTA A. MIGNANI BUDRIO" with the note "FA" [P] ([Carisbo L10](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/ocarina-sopranino-arrigo-mignani-c-1985)). Cesari instruments are also "verniciata in colore bruno", with a gilded circular mark on the mouthpiece reading "ditta CESARI EMILIO" [P] ([Carisbo L1-3](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/trio-d-ocarine-emilio-cesari-c-1954)). Chiesa stamped "G. Chiesa" on the left tip [P] ([Carisbo L6-8](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/tre-ocarine-guido-chiesa-c-1955)).
- Menaglio: the main line is plain red-brown terracotta with a stamp on the neck, and glazed versions exist [S] ([Folk Flute World](https://folkfluteworld.com/reviews/review_of_fabio_ocarina/review_of_fabio_ocarina.html)). He sells a "Sol2 in terracotta ceramicata" (glazed) [P] ([ocarina.it](https://www.ocarina.it/index.html)). His cleaning instruction reads "NON usate alcool o sostanze contenenti alcool" (do not use alcohol); use a tissue with water or a chlorine disinfectant [P] ([ocarina.it/metodo](https://www.ocarina.it/metodo.html)).
- **Tuning devices:** Cesari's "ocarinton" had "tappi per l'accordatura" (tuning plugs) [P] ([Carisbo L4-5](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/due-ocarine-sopranino-emilio-cesari-c-1954)). Mezzetti's instruments had a metal plunger [P] ([Comune](https://www.comune.budrio.bo.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/museo-dellocarina-e-degli-strumenti-in-terracotta)).
- **Voicing:** Italian ocarinas were "outdoor instruments … provided with voicing perhaps excessively wide with brilliant and powerful sound", and "the wide Italian window obtains a sound rich in harmonics" [S] — [Pacchioni interview](https://pureocarinas.com/intervista-giorgio-pacchioni)
- **Hand-making workshop in the 19th-century Emilian tradition** (Pacchioni and Luce Lanzone): "tecniche di costruzione interamente manuali" (entirely manual techniques). It runs in two sessions "in rispetto dei naturali tempi di essiccamento dell'argilla" (respecting the clay's natural drying times), with firing done afterwards once the pieces are completely dry [S] — [Scuola di Musica Popolare di Forlimpopoli](https://www.musicapopolare.net/smp/laboratorio-di-costruzione-dell-ocarina-di-budrio/)
- **Menaglio polymer line:** the Do 3 is "stampata in due pezzi e poi incollati. Se subisce un forte urto, i due pezzi si possono scollare" (made in two pieces and glued; a hard knock can separate them); 17.5 × 7.5 cm, 50 g [D] — [Borsari](https://www.borsarionline.it/fabio-menaglio-ocarina-di-budrio-do-3-cromatica-in-polimero.html). Search extracts say the Sol 2 and Do 1 polymer models are made in one piece [D] — [Sol2](https://www.borsarionline.it/fabio-menaglio-ocarina-di-budrio-sol-2-cromatica-in-polimero.html), [Do1](https://www.borsarionline.it/fabio-menaglio-ocarina-di-budrio-do-1-cromatica-in-polimero.html) (not fetched directly).
- A short video by Menaglio on the history and how the ocarina is made (Italian with English subtitles) is linked by [Campin](http://www.campin.me.uk/Music/Ocarina/): [YouTube lbaewdEH628](http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lbaewdEH628). It could not be viewed from this environment.
- The De.Co. Disciplinare, read in full (7 pp.), contains **no technical production rules**: nothing on clay, moulds, firing, tuning or finish. It is historical and cultural only [P] — [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf)

### Inferences
- A "bilanciere" in Italian workshop usage is a screw press with a weighted balance bar. Combined with "metal moulds", this suggests historic Budrio production pressed clay into rigid (metal) dies, most likely one per half-shell, which were then joined. No Budrio source explicitly describes the two-half joining. Menaglio's two-piece polymer Do 3 is consistent with a two-half body.
- Italian "stampata" means both "moulded/pressed" and "printed", so the Borsari text does not settle whether the polymer ocarina is injection-moulded or 3D-printed.
- "Verniciata in colore bruno" means coated/painted brown, not glazed. Menaglio's warning against alcohol suggests an alcohol-sensitive coating (for example a shellac- or lacquer-type finish). This is unconfirmed.
- The traditional "wide" window/voicing (Pacchioni) is consistent with the project's goals of high projection and a steep blowing curve.
- Plaster press moulds are fine for the project, but they are not the historical Budrio method according to Pacchioni.

### Gaps
- Firing temperature and kiln type, historically and today, for Vicinelli, Mignani and Menaglio: not found.
- Tuning method (before or after firing, tools): not found in a verifiable source. A search-engine summary claimed "holes made at leather-hard for a first tuning, perfected after firing", but I could not locate the source page, so it is unverified.
- Tools for the windway, window and labium (blades, gauges): not found. The likely places to find them are Menaglio's video, the museum's tool collection, and the 1999/2003 catalogues (see Q7).
- Clay body composition (local Budrio brick clay from Fornace Silvani?): not found.

## 3. Budrio size table, settimino, keys and ranges, pitch standard

### Takeaway
The Budrio number is a **size rank in the settimino**, not an octave. Do1 = C6, Sol2 = G5, Do3 = C5, Sol4 = G4, Do5 = C4, Sol6 = G3, Do7 = C3. Menaglio currently lists Do1 through Do5. The settimino dates from 1873 under Vicinelli. Parts are written as if for a C instrument (a transposing convention). No source states the pitch standard (A = 440/442/435); this is an open gap.

| Do 5 | C4–D5 | 8 (pinky hole omitted, too hard to close) | 22 × 13 cm | 735 g | — |

- The museum displays "la serie completa di nove ocarine prodotte oggi da Fabio Menaglio" (the complete series of nine ocarinas Menaglio makes today) [P] — [Comune](https://www.comune.budrio.bo.it/vivere-il-comune/luoghi/museo-dellocarina-e-degli-strumenti-in-terracotta). This does not match the five sizes on his website; the nine are unidentified.
- Consort scheme, low to high: "C7, G6, C5, G4, C3, G2, C1". The lowest note of the C5 is middle C, and the C7 reaches the viola's low C [S] — [Campin](http://www.campin.me.uk/Music/Ocarina/). A Mignani contrabass "Do 7, pitch C3" is 360 mm and 1.8 kg [D] — [VM Collectables](https://vmcollectables.com/product/contrabass-ocarina-mignani/)
- The settimino (seven players) was instituted in 1873 under Vicinelli and "tuttora predomina" (still predominates) [P] — [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf). Folk Flute World's claim that Donati himself classified the settimino is unsourced [S] — [Folk Flute World](https://folkfluteworld.com/reviews/review_of_fabio_ocarina/review_of_fabio_ocarina.html)
- Transposing convention: "La diteggiatura è identica sia per le ocarine in DO che per quelle in SOL. Quando si legge il do basso, si chiudono tutti i fori" (fingering is identical for C and G ocarinas; the written low C closes all holes) [P] — [ocarina.it/metodo](https://www.ocarina.it/metodo.html). Settimino arrangements are "notated as if played on a C ocarina" [S] — [Campin](http://www.campin.me.uk/Music/Ocarina/)
- The range is "fino all'undicesima" (up to an eleventh); chromatic notes come from cross-fingering. Timbre runs "da molto squillante e penetrante nella più piccola, a più scuro e rotondo nella più grande" (very bright and piercing in the smallest, darker and rounder in the largest) [P] — [ocarina.it/storia](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)

### Inferences
- A "Do 3" is by definition the C5 soprano (called "Alto C" in American/Asian naming). The project's target, C5–F6, matches Menaglio's Do 3 and the traditional size rank 3.
- Pitch standard: modern concert makers usually tune to A = 440–442, but there is no evidence for Budrio. Historical 19th-century instruments may be at older pitches. The value should be measured on the reference Mignani.

### Gaps
- Pitch standard (440/442/435) for Vicinelli, Mignani or Menaglio: **not found**.
- The historic Budrio size table with dimensions per size (a Vicinelli/Mignani "tabella"): not found online.

## 4. Budrio 10-hole fingering vs Asian 12-hole; reported playing characteristics

### Takeaway
The Italian system has 10 holes (8 on top, 2 thumbs) and no subholes. The high notes run D = left thumb off, E = **left pinky** off, F = all open. This forces a **large left-pinky hole** (9 mm oval on the Menaglio Do 3). Players describe Menaglio instruments as loud, very dynamic, needing a lot of air and full pressure on high notes, with a large low-to-high pressure difference, but with **low backpressure** (resistance).

### Cited Findings
- In the Italian system, E is played by lifting the pinky, where the Asian system lifts the right thumb. This keeps the instrument supported longer but "makes the pinky hole a lot bigger". 12-hole instruments add two subholes that extend the range three semitones down [S] — [Pure Ocarinas: fingering](https://pureocarinas.com/ocarina-fingering-system); [Pure Ocarinas: 10 vs 12](https://pureocarinas.com/differance-10-hole-12-hole-ocarina) (search extracts)
- Fingering table: low C# is a half-hole on the right little finger; Italian E = "----- t----" (left pinky off, right thumb on); "the left-hand little finger hole is larger than the right thumbhole in the Italian system" [S] — [Campin](http://www.campin.me.uk/Music/Ocarina/)
- On the Menaglio Do 3: low C# needs half-holing; high E and some chromatic notes differ from 12-hole fingering. Reviewers describe "quick responsiveness" and "lovely dulcet tones" [S] — [Folk Flute World](https://folkfluteworld.com/reviews/review_of_fabio_ocarina/review_of_fabio_ocarina.html)
- Forum reports [F] (search extracts from threads including [Menaglio Do 3 review t7074](https://theocarinanetwork.com/fabio-menaglio-do-3-alto-c-review-t7074.html), [Backpressure t8292](https://theocarinanetwork.com/backpressure-t8292.html), [A good Budrio style ocarina? t7918](https://theocarinanetwork.com/a-good-budrio-style-ocarina-t7918.html)):
  - "the most dynamic ocarinas are made by Fabio Menaglio … medium loudness on the low notes and very loud on high notes … significant difference in breath pressure required for low notes and for high notes"
  - "require a lot of breath, especially on the high notes … backpressure is very low"
  - "solid, reliable intonation"
  - "not recommended to beginners"; without breath control they can "sound airy"
- Pressure sensitivity: "most notes can be varied in pitch by about a tone by blowing harder or softer" [S] — [Campin](http://www.campin.me.uk/Music/Ocarina/)

### Inferences
- The Budrio/Menaglio profile of a steep curve, very loud high notes and high airflow matches the project's goals of a steep curve and high projection. "High breath pressure" should be read as a high *required pressure* on top notes, not as high *resistance*. The traditional design has low resistance, meaning a wide windway and window.
- For the Do 3 master, the left-pinky hole must be the second-largest opening (it gives the D→E whole tone), and roughly oval so it fits under the fingertip.

### Gaps
- No player reports specific to **Mignani** instruments (tone, pressure, intonation) were found. Forum threads could not be opened.

## 5. Published dimensions and photos of Mignani / Menaglio / Vicinelli Do 3 and related instruments

### Takeaway
Measured data exists for the Menaglio Do 3 (two slightly different sets of numbers) and for Tagliavini museum pieces by Cesari, Chiesa and Mignani. No Vicinelli dimensions and no Mignani Do 3 dimensions were found. The Tagliavini catalogue appears to use the **Franco-Italian octave convention (Do3 = middle C)**, so the Mignani "Fa4" sopranino is probably F5 in scientific notation.

### Cited Findings
- **Menaglio Do 3 (terracotta):** 17.5 × 8.5 cm, 215 g, 9 mm oval left-pinky hole [P] — [ocarina.it](https://www.ocarina.it/index.html). A reviewer measured length 159 mm, width 88.5 mm, height 38 mm [S] — [Folk Flute World](https://folkfluteworld.com/reviews/review_of_fabio_ocarina/review_of_fabio_ocarina.html). The two lengths conflict (175 vs 159 mm).
- **Menaglio Do 3 (polymer):** 17.5 × 7.5 cm, 50 g [D] — [Borsari](https://www.borsarionline.it/fabio-menaglio-ocarina-di-budrio-do-3-cromatica-in-polimero.html)
- **Tagliavini collection** (*Collezione Tagliavini. Catalogo degli strumenti musicali*, eds. J.H. van der Meer and L.F. Tagliavini, Bononia University Press, 2008, vol. II, pp. 250–257) [P]:
  - **L1-3**, Cesari, Sanremo c.1954: trio of 12, 18 and 24 cm. Sopranino in Do5 (10 holes), "d'armonia" in Do4 + Sol4 (5 + 5 holes), bassetto in Do3 (8 holes) — [link](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/trio-d-ocarine-emilio-cesari-c-1954)
  - **L4-5**, Cesari c.1954: two sopranino "ocarinton" in Do5, 13 (13.3) × 5.8 cm, 10 holes, with tuning plugs — [link](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/due-ocarine-sopranino-emilio-cesari-c-1954)
  - **L6-8**, Chiesa, Budrio c.1955: 13, 17 and 20 cm in Si4, Re4 and Fa3, with 10, 10 and 9 holes. The structured record fields wrongly name "Emilio Cesari" as maker — [link](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/tre-ocarine-guido-chiesa-c-1955)
  - **L10**, Mignani, c.1985: sopranino "in Fa4", 19 × 7.7 × 4 cm, 10 holes (8 + 2), coated brown — [link](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/ocarina-sopranino-arrigo-mignani-c-1985)
  - **L9**: no online record found.
- **Mignani contrabass Do 7 (C3):** 360 mm, 1,800 g, "246" marked on the voicing [D] — [VM Collectables](https://vmcollectables.com/product/contrabass-ocarina-mignani/)
- Photos of a mid-20th-century Mignani ocarina and a Menaglio "do-3" appear on [Campin](http://www.campin.me.uk/Music/Ocarina/) (no scale given).

### Inferences
- **Octave convention.** Matching Tagliavini lengths against Menaglio's sizes:
  - Cesari bassetto "Do3", 24 cm, 8 holes ≈ Menaglio Do 5 (C4), 22 cm, 8 holes.
  - Sopranino "Do5", 12–13 cm ≈ Menaglio Do 1 (C6), 12.5 cm.
  - Chiesa "Fa3", 20 cm ≈ F4 in SPN (Menaglio Sol 4, G4, is 19 cm).
  
  This consistently implies Do3 = C4 (middle C). Mignani's "Fa4" sopranino would then be **F5 in SPN**. Its 7.7 cm width sits between the Menaglio Sol 2 (6.5 cm) and Do 3 (8.5 cm), which fits F5; its 19 cm length does not, which suggests Mignani's shapes were more elongated. The project's note calling it "F4" should be corrected or at least flagged.
- Proportions of the Menaglio Do 3 are about 2.06 : 1 length to width (from 17.5 × 8.5) and a height of about 3.8 cm (FFW). These are usable initial envelope targets.

### Gaps
- No Vicinelli dimensions and no Mignani **Do 3** dimensions, hole diameters or internal volume were found. The Catawiki Mignani "in Fa" (1968) listing was blocked.
- Hole diameters other than the left pinky, and window/windway dimensions, are unpublished.

## 8. Legal protection of the name or design

### Takeaway
Since April 2024 the "Ocarina di Budrio" holds a **De.Co.** (Denominazione Comunale d'Origine) under the Comune/Città metropolitana di Bologna scheme. It was awarded to five cultural associations, not to a maker. Its Disciplinare has no technical specification. No trademark or geographical indication was found.

### Cited Findings
- The De.Co. was awarded to Gruppo Ocarinistico Budriese APS, Il Temporale APS, Società Cooperativa Sociale Arcobaleno, Fondazione Entroterre and Associazione Diapason Progetti Musicali APS; published 24-04-2024 [P] — [Bologna Metropolitana](https://www.bolognametropolitana.it/Archivio_news/001/L_ocarina_di_Budrio_ottiene_la_DeCo); [DeCo Bologna](https://www.decobologna.it/objects/ocarina-di-budrio)
- The Disciplinare's working group was G.P. Borghi (ethnographer, coordinator), E. Bernagozzi, F. Galliani and D. Staiti (organologist, DAMS). Its content is history, groups, bibliography and discography only [P] — [Disciplinare](https://assets.culturabologna.it/99efa27e-8fc8-40e1-a4e4-21564163b786-disciplinare-ocarina-di-budrio.pdf)
- The ocarina is described as "patrimonio culturale della Regione Emilia-Romagna" (cultural heritage of the Emilia-Romagna region) [P] — same source

### Inferences
- A De.Co. is a municipal promotional label. It does not restrict the shape or the making of Budrio-style ocarinas elsewhere. A privately made instrument sold abroad should avoid calling itself "Ocarina di Budrio" or using Menaglio's marks; "Budrio-style" / "stile Budrio" is descriptive.

### Gaps
- No UIBM/EUIPO trademark search was done, and no check was made for an EU craft geographical indication application. Both remain unverified.
