## 1. Books and documents on ocarina making (Hickman, other languages, theses, historic Italian texts)

### Takeaway
Hickman's *The Art of Ocarina Making* is still the only complete, current, mould-aware making manual in English. It covers aligned plaster moulds, voicing, breath curve, 10-hole tuning and finishing. Among free sources, the most useful match for this workflow is Kenji Ogawa's Japanese photo walkthrough of **pressing clay slabs into two-part plaster moulds**. For the Budrio/Mignani specifics, the best printed sources are the Tagliavini catalogue (already known) and Aldo Adversi's 1963 monograph. I found no academic thesis on ocarina *making*.

**Japanese sources**
- **Kenji Ogawa, fl-oca.com, 「ある日の工房を見学すると」 ("型を使っての作り方", mould-based method)** [fetched, Shift-JIS text read directly] — [fl-oca.com/hmo/hmo.htm](https://www.fl-oca.com/hmo/hmo.htm). The page shows a **press-mould** process, not slip casting:
  - Roll slabs to a fixed thickness between タタラ板 (thickness guides) and cut them to fit the mould.
  - Press the slab into each plaster half and force fine detail against the plaster.
  - Trim the excess clay with the blunt side of a knife held against the plaster.
  - Use a spatula shaped to the mould to set the wall thickness near the head, and add extra clay in the windway area, pushing from inside.
  - Score the joining faces with a toothpick comb, brush on slip (ドベ), then close the two moulds and press the mouthpiece.
  - Insert the windway spatula "at the correct angle" (角度に注意) and mark its tip.
  - Dry for about half a day, then de-burr and punch the tone holes.
  - Open the windway with a thin spatula, then a finishing spatula. Repair the blow-out with a 4 mm punch, and cut the edge and the underside of the labium.
  - Tune, finish the surface, tune again, and then dry fully.
  - Bisque fire. Optional black-ware (黒陶) firing in saggars.
  - The page warns that black-ware firing in an electric kiln can cause heater-element failure.
  - It says moulds are used "not for mass production but to control pitch" (量産のためでなく音程をコントロールする為).
- Same author, English pages: ["Making of an Ocarina"](https://www.fl-oca.com/eng/egm.htm) (hand-modelled introduction: 7 mm walls, boxwood windway spatula, 7 mm punch for the sound hole) [fetched]; ["The Principles of the Ocarina"](https://www.fl-oca.com/eng/ocarina1.htm) [snippet].
- SUELABO (美濃焼 Mino-ware ocarina workshop) publishes its production steps [fetched] — [sue-labo.com/ocarina](https://sue-labo.com/ocarina):
  - slip casting (clay + water + sodium silicate) into plaster moulds for the upper and lower halves
  - fine tone-critical work before joining with ドベ (slip)
  - a first tuning before firing, based on repeated tests of how much the pitch shifts in firing
  - firing temperature kept proprietary
  - sanding and individual tuning with a tuner after firing

**Italian / Budrio historical texts**

### Inferences
- For a Budrio Do3 copy, the useful stack is: Hickman for windway/labium geometry and the 10-hole tuning method; Ogawa's page for the actual press-mould sequence (slab thickness, adding clay at the windway, when to insert the spatula); and the Tagliavini catalogue plus the Carisbo digital records for Mignani dimensions and markings. Adversi's book matters for history and context, not technique.
- The print ISBN edition (2012) may be older than the 2019-updated PDF, so the PDF from ocarinamaking.com is the better buy.

### Takeaway
Hands-on *making* courses are rare and short. In Italy there is Giorgio Pacchioni's 2-day hand-built Budrio laboratorio. In Austria, Kurt Posch runs 2-day building courses for his "Modell Budrio". In Japan, SUELABO offers a plaster-mould maker experience. Korea is dominated by *playing* certificates. Brazil has only one-off hand-built ocarina workshops (e.g., Florianópolis). ## 6. Health & safety for this workflow (concise, authoritative)

### Takeaway
The dominant chronic hazard is **respirable crystalline silica** from dried clay, plaster/clay mould handling and sanding fired terracotta. The controls are to keep everything damp, clean with water or a Class M/H vacuum, never sweep dry or use compressed air, and wear a P2/PFF2 respirator for dusty tasks. Other hazards are plaster heat (never cast body parts), PLA printer emissions (ventilate or enclose), kiln gases, and **lead**: use only lead-free materials for a mouth-contact instrument. Treat the antique's varnish as unknown and fragile.

### Cited Findings
**Crystalline silica (clay, slip, moulds, fired-terracotta dust)**
- HSE COSHH Essentials sheet **CR2 "Casting"** (ceramics, silica) [fetched] — [hse.gov.uk CR2 PDF](https://www.hse.gov.uk/PUBNS/guidance/cr2.pdf):
  - Respirable crystalline silica (RCS) is "very fine and invisible under normal lighting".
  - It causes silicosis (irreversible), increased lung-cancer risk in people with silicosis, and COPD. It can also cause contact dermatitis.
  - "Raised dust levels result from slip and clay drying out"; "keep surfaces clean – never let slip or clay spills dry out"; "remember that handling used moulds creates dust".
  - "Vacuum dry dust or use wet cleaning methods", with a vacuum of "at least dust Class M"; "clean surfaces with water and a sponge"; "wash down the workroom at the end of each day".
  - "Never allow the use of brushes or compressed air for removing dust from skin and clothing."
  - Store clean and contaminated clothing separately. Wash hands before eating or drinking.
  - RPE is "normally not needed" for casting itself but "may be needed for maintenance and cleaning".
  - The same series includes sheet CR0, a table of silica concentrations in common materials.
- US OSHA's silica standard sets a **PEL of 50 µg/m³** (8-h TWA) and an **action level of 25 µg/m³**. [snippet, standard page] — [OSHA 29 CFR 1910.1053](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.1053); [OSHA 3682 fact sheet](https://www.osha.gov/sites/default/files/publications/OSHA3682.pdf)
- University EHS guidance [fetched] — [WCU ceramics safety](https://www.wcu.edu/discover/campus-services-and-operations/facilities-management/safety-and-risk-management/visual-arts-theatre-safety/ceramics-safety.aspx):
  - clean the studio daily by wet-mopping
  - "dry sweeping and vacuuming must be avoided unless a HEPA filter vacuum is used"
  - use a wet mop for powder spills
- Australian government guidance for pottery: respirators and/or **P2** disposable masks must fit correctly. [snippet] — [asbestossafety.gov.au – Pottery and ceramics](https://www.asbestossafety.gov.au/silicasafety/about-silica/practical-guidance/pottery-and-ceramics)
- **Brazil**
  - NR-15 Anexo 12 limits depend on the quartz percentage: respirable dust = **8 / (%quartzo + 2) mg/m³**; total dust = **24 / (%quartzo + 3) mg/m³**. [snippet, secondary] — [NR-15 Anexo 12 (guiatrabalhista PDF)](https://www.guiatrabalhista.com.br/legislacao/nr/nr-15-anexo-12.pdf); [HO Fácil](https://hofacil.com.br/blog/quais-os-limites-de-tolerancia-para-poeira-mineral-em-ambiente-de-trabalho/)
  - Fundacentro coordinates the **Programa Nacional de Eliminação da Silicose (PNES)**, begun in 2002. [snippet] — [gov.br/fundacentro](https://www.gov.br/fundacentro/pt-br/comunicacao/noticias/noticias/2004/6/eliminacao-da-silicose-e-objetivo-de-programa-nacional-da-fundacentro)
  - Fundacentro's silica page describes silicosis as irreversible with no treatment. It stresses primary prevention by stopping dust formation and dispersion, and lists wet working/humidification, ventilation and **PFF2** respirators. [fetched] — [Fundacentro – Sílica e Silicose](https://www.gov.br/fundacentro/pt-br/acesso-a-informacao/acoes-e-programas/projetos-encerrados/silica-e-silicose/silica-e-silicose)
  - A **0.025 mg/m³ "valor-guia"** attributed to Fundacentro appears only on a secondary site, not on the Fundacentro page I read. Treat it as unverified. [snippet] — [protecaorespiratoria.com](https://protecaorespiratoria.com/como-a-silica-livre-cristalizada-atua-dentro-dos-pulmoes/)

**Plaster (mould making)**
- NSEAD (UK art-education H&S) [fetched] — [NSEAD – Plaster of Paris](https://www.nsead.org/resources/health-safety/h-s-materials/hs-plaster-of-paris/):
  - the dust is an irritant; use a scoop to limit dust
  - setting can reach "**as high as 60 °C**", and "skin damage can result… perhaps as low as 45 °C if contact is prolonged"; use cold water
  - "Hands, fingers, parts of the body should **never** be submerged into liquid mixed plaster… and held there until the plaster hardens"
  - for eye contact, rinse with plenty of water for several minutes
- The 1982 *Burns* case report "Heat generation in plaster-of-Paris and resulting hand burns": four pupils made hand moulds with dental plaster, and three needed finger amputations. The plaster reached about 70 °C. [snippet] — [PubMed 7172075](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7172075/); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/0305417982901358); [Barnet H&S Bulletin 193](https://www.barnet.gov.uk/sites/default/files/assets/workingwithchildren/healthandsafetybulletins/193plasterofparis.pdf)

**PLA 3D printing emissions**
- UL Chemical Insights [fetched] — [chemicalinsights.ul.org/3d-printing](https://chemicalinsights.ul.org/3d-printing/):
  - FFF printers emit ultrafine particles (<100 nm), "up to one trillion particles per hour", and "often exceeding 200 distinct" VOCs.
  - "PLA generally emits fewer contaminants" than ABS or nylon.
  - Controls: local exhaust to outdoors; a **ventilated enclosure cut particles by 99.7% and TVOC by 69.5%**; HEPA + activated-carbon enclosures cut peak particles by ≥95%; limit time spent near the printer.
  - The emissions test standard is **ANSI/CAN/UL 2904**. See also the UL report [PDF](https://chemicalinsights.ul.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/R360_3DP_081624.pdf).
- Peer-reviewed sources: *Environ. Sci. Technol.* 50(3):1260 (2016), "Emissions of Ultrafine Particles and VOCs from Commercially Available Desktop 3D Printers with Multiple Filaments" [snippet] — [ACS](https://pubs.acs.org/esthag/article/50/3/1260/825584/Emissions-of-Ultrafine-Particles-and-Volatile); chamber-data consolidation in *Environment International* (2023) [snippet] — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0160412023005895)
  - Nozzle temperature and nozzle diameter strongly affect emissions.
  - Ordinary dust masks do not filter UFPs or VOCs.

**Kiln firing**
- Early bisque firing oxidises organic matter to **carbon monoxide** and other combustion gases. Later, sulfur compounds give "highly irritating sulfur oxides", plus nitrogen oxides. Fuel kilns vent through a chimney. [fetched] — [WCU](https://www.wcu.edu/discover/campus-services-and-operations/facilities-management/safety-and-risk-management/visual-arts-theatre-safety/ceramics-safety.aspx)
- Kiln rooms should have CO monitors. Formaldehyde has been measured near electric kilns. A direct-to-outdoors kiln vent is recommended. [snippet] — [The Ceramic School](https://ceramic.school/home-studio-safety/); [Kiln Control blog](https://www.kilncontrol.com/blog/kiln-safety-at-home-kilns/)
- Firing black-ware (reduction/carbon) in an electric kiln can damage heater elements (a maker's note). [fetched] — [fl-oca.com/hmo](https://www.fl-oca.com/hmo/hmo.htm)

**Finishes for a mouth-contact instrument**
- Digitalfire [fetched] — [digitalfire.com – Lead in ceramic glazes](https://digitalfire.com/glossary/lead+in+ceramic+glazes); see also [Are your glazes food safe?](https://digitalfire.com/article/12):

  - traditional low-fire terracotta pottery (e.g., Mexican) "is all lead-glazed"
  - hobbyists should avoid lead entirely
- "Use lead-free glazes. If the glaze does not state 'lead-free' or 'leadless'… assume it contains lead." [fetched] — [WCU](https://www.wcu.edu/discover/campus-services-and-operations/facilities-management/safety-and-risk-management/visual-arts-theatre-safety/ceramics-safety.aspx)
- US FDA compliance policy on lead in ceramic foodware. [snippet] — [FDA CPG Sec. 545.450](https://www.fda.gov/files/inspections,%20compliance,%20enforcement,%20and%20criminal%20investigations/published/CPG-Sec.-545.450-Pottery-(Ceramics)--Import-and-Domestic---Lead-Contamination.pdf)
- Pure Ocarinas on finishes [fetched] — [pureocarinas.com – materials & finish](https://pureocarinas.com/ocarina-materials-finish-differences):
  - ocarinas come with "fired glazes, natural shellac, and plain ceramic"; "synthetic finishes including lacquers and epoxy are also becoming more common"
  - shellac is "pretty soft, but has other ergonomic advantages"
  - "Never use alcohol-based cleaners on shellac finished ocarinas"; "re-firing a shellac-finished ocarina will remove the finish"

- Shellac is an edible glazing agent (**E904**, "confectioner's/pharmaceutical glaze") used on pills, sweets and fruit, dissolved in ethanol. [fetched] — [Wikipedia – Shellac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shellac)
- A forum post warns that water-soluble acrylic paint is also saliva-soluble. [snippet] — [The Ocarina Network – DIY ocarina painting](https://theocarinanetwork.com/diy-ocarina-painting-t20645.html)

**Antique varnished reference instrument**
- Mignani instruments in the Tagliavini collection are **terracotta with a brown varnish**. [fetched] — [Fondazione Carisbo](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/ocarina-sopranino-arrigo-mignani-c-1985)
- CIMCIM publishes conservation and copying guidance (Publication No. 3, 1994, *Copies of Historic Musical Instruments*), but the PDF is no longer reachable. [fetched: dead link] — [CIMCIM](https://cimcim.mini.icom.museum/conservation-of-musical-instruments/)

### Inferences (practical rules derived from the sources above; not verbatim guidance)
- **Clay/plaster studio:** keep trimmings and slip wet and bagged. Wipe benches with a damp sponge. Mop the floor. Never sweep dry or blow dust. Wear an apron or dedicated clothes and wash them separately. Use a **PFF2 (≈EN FFP2/P2) respirator** when mixing dry plaster or clay powders, scraping dried clay off moulds, or cleaning up.
- **Sanding or rotary-tool tuning of fired terracotta** is a silica-dust task. Work wet where possible (misting or a wet sponge), under local extraction or outdoors, wearing PFF2 and safety glasses. For the eye-protection point I found no specific source; it is standard practice.
- **Plaster:** add plaster to cold water, mix in a ventilated area, never immerse hands. The setting heat (up to ~60–70 °C per the sources) could also soften a **PLA master**, since PLA's glass transition is roughly 55–65 °C. This is an inference from general polymer data I did not verify in this research. Seal and release-coat the master, and prefer thin shells or several smaller pours.
- **PLA printing:** print in a ventilated room, not a bedroom. Ideally use an enclosure vented outdoors or fitted with HEPA+carbon filters, print at the lowest reliable temperature, and leave the room during long prints.
- **Kiln (rented):** stay out of the kiln room during burn-off (roughly the first several hundred °C). Make sure there is a hood or vent and a CO alarm. Use heat-resistant gloves and a face shield when opening the kiln. For IR shade lenses when looking into a hot kiln, I found no verified source; it is standard pottery advice.
- **Finishes:** for a mouthpiece and windway, the safest options are (1) leave it **unglazed** (traditional Budrio terracotta) or (2) use food-grade (E904) dewaxed shellac cut with pure ethanol, then let it cure fully. Avoid any glaze or pigment not documented as lead-free, and avoid water-based paints or varnishes near the lips. Do not copy the antique's varnish without knowing its composition.
- **Handling the antique Mignani:** use clean nitrile gloves (or clean, dry hands) and a padded surface. Do not apply adhesive tape or graphite rubbing to the varnished surface: tape can lift degraded varnish and leave residue, and graphite can abrade or stain. Prefer non-contact methods (photogrammetry or photos with a scale bar), calipers with protected jaws, or a contour gauge with film interleaving. Ask the owner or museum before any contact method. Every point in this bullet is an inference; I could not retrieve the CIMCIM or conservation texts.

## 7. IP / legal status of copying a historic Budrio / Mignani design for personal use

### Takeaway
For a private, non-commercial copy, I found no IP barrier. The Budrio form dates from 1853 (Donati). Mignani took over Cesari's existing equipment rather than creating a new design. Registered design protection is capped at 25 years in Brazil and has a private-use exception. "Ocarina di Budrio" carries a municipal **De.Co.**, a local-origin quality label granted to Budrio associations. This concerns naming and marketing, not the act of making one. Do not reproduce Mignani's maker's stamp, and do not sell the copy as a "Budrio" or "Mignani" ocarina.

### Cited Findings
- The ocarina di Budrio was created in 1853 by Giuseppe Donati, then aged 17. It received the **De.Co. (Denominazione Comunale d'Origine) on 24 April 2024**. The mark was recognised to Gruppo Ocarinistico Budriese APS, Il Temporale APS, Coop. Arcobaleno, Fondazione Entroterre and Associazione Diapason, as a guarantee of authenticity and provenance. [snippet] — [Città metropolitana di Bologna](https://www.cittametropolitana.bo.it/portale/Home/Archivio_news/L_ocarina_di_Budrio_ottiene_la_DeCo); [Ocarina Rave](https://www.ocarinarave.it/2024/04/29/l-ocarina-di-budrio-ottiene-il-marchio-de-co-di-denominazione-comunale-d-origine/); [Legacoop Bologna](https://www.legacoop.bologna.it/locarina-di-budrio-ottiene-il-marchio-di-denominazione-comunale-dorigine/04/2024/)
- Mignani (d. 1997) bought the equipment of the late builder **Emilio Cesari** from Cesari's heirs and continued production for 28 years. [fetched] — [ocarina.it/storia.html](https://www.ocarina.it/storia.html)
- His instruments carry the stamp "DITTA A. MIGNANI / BUDRIO". [fetched] — [Fondazione Carisbo](https://digital.fondazionecarisbo.it/artwork/ocarina-sopranino-arrigo-mignani-c-1985)
- In Italian copyright law, industrial designs are protected only if they "presentino di per sé **carattere creativo e valore artistico**" (L. 633/1941, art. 2 n. 10). [fetched] — [Brocardi](https://www.brocardi.it/legge-diritto-autore/titolo-i/capo-i/art2.html)
- In Brazilian law (Lei 9.279/1996, LPI) [fetched] — [Planalto – Lei 9.279/96](https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/leis/l9279.htm):
  - A *desenho industrial* must be new and original (art. 95–96).
  - Registration lasts **10 years, extendable by 3 × 5 years (max 25)** (art. 108).
  - Rights arise only from registration (art. 109).
  - Art. 109, sole paragraph, applies art. 43 I to designs: rights do not reach "atos praticados por terceiros não autorizados, **em caráter privado e sem finalidade comercial**, desde que não acarretem prejuízo ao interesse econômico do titular".

### Inferences
- A Brazilian design registration for the Budrio shape is implausible. Even if one existed, private non-commercial making is excepted by art. 43 I via art. 109.
- Italian copyright is unlikely to cover a traditional 19th-century functional form: it would need "creative character and artistic value", and the form predates Mignani.
- The De.Co. is a municipal promotional label, not a registered trademark that I could verify. Its practical effect is on *using the name* to market products.
- Avoid copying Mignani's stamp or "M" mark, which would suggest passing off, and label the copy as "after Mignani / Budrio type".

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the De.Co. *disciplinare* (production rules) or check whether "Ocarina di Budrio" is registered as a trademark at UIBM or EUIPO. That needs direct searches of those registries.
- The Italian copyright duration for industrial design (generally 70 years after the author's death) was not verified in this research. If it applied to Mignani (d. 1997), it would run to about 2067, but applicability is doubtful.
- This is not legal advice. Confirm before any sale.
